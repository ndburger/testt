Organizing Agile Teams by Product, Not Project

Many of my roles have involved fixing how teams are organized, not just what they build. This post collects lessons from earlier roles at StoneX Group and Ruan Transportation, and from my current role as a Senior Product Manager at Logiwa, where I consulted product and engineering leadership to redesign organizational structure and agile development processes, reducing release bug counts and accelerating deployment timelines.

Align Teams to Products

At StoneX as an Agile Business Analyst, I developed and presented a reorganization plan to management to streamline operations while controlling intake and priority by aligning teams by product rather than project. It decreased support ticket escalations by 15%. When teams form and disband around projects, nobody owns the product after launch. A product-aligned team keeps context, owns quality, and receives its own intake.

Control Intake and Priority

A team can only be effective if it knows what it works on next. Define one intake path, one priority order, and a clear owner for the backlog. I applied this idea at Logiwa as well, where I led client-facing product engagement across North America by collecting structured feedback, aligning the product roadmap with customer needs, and streamlining the intake process for faster prioritization and delivery.

Practice the Roles You Coach

At StoneX I operated as a Scrum Master and Product Owner while partnering with stakeholders on refactoring a commodity price tracking application into a web scraper, saving hours of daily manual data entry. Playing both roles clarifies the difference between them: the Product Owner decides what and why, and the Scrum Master protects the team's process.

Coach Teams, Not Just Tools

At Ruan Transportation, as a Business Systems Analyst, I coached 3 standing teams on best practices for Jira and Agile methodologies while overseeing a team of 4. I bridged gaps between support and development teams, which decreased ticket resolution times by 50%. Tooling matters less than shared habits: how tickets are written, how handoffs happen, and how support issues reach developers.

Give Technology Decisions a Home

Also at Ruan, I established a Technology Governance Board, which saved over $50,000 in IT costs while I advised the Chief Information Officer on new technology strategies, including Internet of Things, Blockchain, and Data Lakes. A governance forum with clear criteria prevents duplicate spending and gives leadership one place to evaluate new technology.

Takeaways

  • Organize around products so someone owns outcomes after launch.
  • Give each team one intake path and one priority order.
  • Understand Scrum Master and Product Owner by doing both.
  • Create a forum for technology decisions.
