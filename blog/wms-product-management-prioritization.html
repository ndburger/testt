WMS Product Management: Prioritize by Operational Impact

Warehouse software has an unusual feedback loop: when a feature is wrong, the cost shows up on the warehouse floor in shipping accuracy, throughput, and inventory visibility. My background in this space spans Körber Supply Chain, where I was a Solutions Consultant, and Flexport, where as a Program Analyst I partnered with operations and engineering to improve warehouse management system performance across 30+ e-commerce warehouses. This post covers practices that matter for a WMS product manager.

Prioritize by Operational Impact

At Flexport, I authored product requirement documentation and prioritized projects by operational impact, improving on-time shipping accuracy and merchant inventory visibility. Operational impact is a useful lens because it ties backlog items to outcomes warehouse teams already care about:

  • On-time shipping
  • Inventory accuracy and visibility
  • Labor efficiency and throughput
  • Exception volume and support load

Write Requirements for Operations and Engineering

A good WMS requirements document is readable by an operations lead and buildable by an engineer. Describe the workflow as it happens on the floor, name the edge cases, and state the acceptance criteria as observable behavior. The same document should make clear which outcome it is meant to improve.

Use Data to See What the Warehouse Is Doing

At Körber, I created a business intelligence portfolio and training documentation consisting of over 25 SQL reports and analytical dashboards, which gave clients guidance on warehouse operations performance. Reporting is part of the product in a WMS. Operators need to see where work is stuck, and a PM needs the same data to judge whether a change helped.

Plan Integrations as Programs

When Deliverr and Shopify networks were integrated, I managed the work across 8 cross-functional development teams to ensure all major milestones were met. Integrations involve many owners at once, so the useful PM work is keeping a shared milestone list, surfacing cross-team dependencies early, and being clear about what must be true for each milestone to count as done.

Automate Support Load Away

At Flexport as a Product Manager II, I launched automation features that reduced customer support tickets by at least 10%, and centralized the support process from four channels into one while managing 15+ stakeholders and vendor negotiations with three enterprise partners. Support tickets are a signal about product gaps, so reducing them is both a customer win and a way to see what to build next.

Takeaways

  • Rank work by its effect on shipping, inventory, and throughput.
  • Write requirements in the language of the warehouse floor.
  • Treat reporting as a core feature.
  • Run integrations as programs with explicit milestones and owners.
